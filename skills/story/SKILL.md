## The job

A story is the one format where the reader has to stay to the end for it
to do anything. It earns that with specifics and with something at stake.
A "story" that is a list of achievements in past tense is a CV.

## Rules

1. **Someone has to want something.** Name the person and what they were
   trying to get. No want, no story — only a sequence of events.
2. **Something has to be in the way.** The obstacle is real, named, and
   was genuinely hard. A story where the obstacle was mild is the kind
   nobody repeats.
3. **Open in the middle of it.** First line: a moment, a scene, a number,
   a thing said out loud. Never "In today's fast-paced world" and never
   "Founded in 2009, the company has always believed".
4. **Specifics carry the weight.** Not "a large order" — "an 18-tonne
   order due the Friday before Diwali". Not "we struggled" — the thing
   that actually happened that week. One concrete detail beats three
   adjectives, always.
5. **Show the turn.** There is a moment where it changes — a decision, a
   realisation, a phone call. Put the reader in that moment rather than
   reporting it afterwards.
6. **Somebody speaks.** At least one line of real dialogue or a quoted
   sentence. It is what makes a case study read as true.
7. **Earn the ending.** The close follows from the turn. No moral, no
   "and that is why we are passionate about quality". If the point needs
   stating, the story did not work.
8. **Past tense, active, short sentences at the turn.** Long sentences
   for setting, short ones where it matters.
9. **Never invent a fact about a real person or company.** Where a detail
   is missing, write around it or mark it clearly for the person to fill
   in. A fabricated quote in a customer story is the worst failure here.

## Shape

Four movements, not four headed sections — the headings are for you, not
for the reader:

```
OPEN     The moment. 2–4 sentences, in scene.
WANT     Who, what they were after, why it mattered to them.
WALL     What got in the way, and what it cost. The longest movement.
TURN     The decision or realisation, in the moment it happened.
LAND     What changed, in a concrete result. One line, then stop.
```

Length: 250 to 500 words for a brand or customer story, 600 to 900 for a
case study with results. Say the word count at the end.

## Quality checklist

- The first sentence is a moment, not a definition of the industry.
- A named person wants something specific by a specific time.
- The obstacle is named and its cost is concrete.
- At least three details are specific enough to be checked (a figure, a
  date, a place, a part, a name).
- At least one quoted line of speech.
- The ending states what changed, not what the reader should feel.
- Nothing about a real person or company is invented; anything unknown is
  marked for the person to supply.

## Non-goals

Do not write marketing copy with a story shape bolted on — no feature
lists, no "our solution", no closing call to action unless the request
asked for one. Do not moralise at the end. Do not use "journey",
"passion", "game-changer" or "transformative" anywhere.
