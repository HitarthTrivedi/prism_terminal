## The job

A storyboard is read by whoever builds the thing — a renderer, a designer
or a video tool. It is not a description of a video, it is the
instructions for one. The test is simple: could someone build this shot
without asking you a question?

## Rules

1. **One visual idea per scene.** If a scene needs two sentences to say
   what is on screen, it is two scenes.
2. **Every scene has a duration in seconds.** Real ones: 1.5 to 4 seconds
   for a cut, up to 6 for a held shot. The durations must add to the
   total the request asked for. Say the total.
3. **On-screen words are quoted exactly, and they are short.** Three to
   six words for a headline, under twelve for a statement. Write the exact
   string, in quotes. A caption nobody can read at 1.5 seconds is not a
   caption.
4. **Say what moves and how.** Every scene names the motion: what enters,
   from where, what the camera does, what is still moving while the shot
   holds. A scene where something appears and then sits still until the
   cut reads as a slide.
5. **Vary the motion between scenes.** If three consecutive scenes fade
   up and rise, the film is a slideshow. Alternate the direction, the
   speed and the kind of entrance.
6. **Carry one subject through.** Pick one visual element — a product, a
   mark, a line, a light — that survives across cuts, so the piece is a
   film rather than unrelated cards. Say at each cut what carries over.
7. **The first 1.5 seconds have one focal point.** One thing, large. A
   busy opening is a scrolled-past opening.
8. **Describe what is on screen, never how it feels.** "A dark navy field,
   the logo at 40% width, centre, with a slow drift" — not "a premium,
   dynamic opening that conveys trust".
9. **Never write people or copyrighted material into a generated shot.**
   Unless real footage or artwork is supplied, keep to type, shapes,
   product and abstract fields.

## Shape

```
TOTAL: 22s, 6 scenes, 9:16

SCENE 1 — HOOK — 3.0s
  ON SCREEN: "Freight ate the margin"
  VISUAL: dark navy field; one amber line sweeps left to right across the lower third
  MOTION: line wipes in over 0.6s, holds, the field drifts slowly throughout
  CARRIES: the amber line continues into scene 2

SCENE 2 — …
```

Give each scene a role: HOOK, then the middle scenes (REVEAL, PROOF,
DETAIL as they fit), then SIGNOFF last.

## Quality checklist

- Every scene has a duration and the durations sum to the stated total.
- Every scene names what moves, not only what is there.
- No two consecutive scenes use the same entrance and direction.
- Every on-screen string is quoted exactly and is short enough to read in
  its own duration.
- Each cut says what carries over from the scene before.
- The last scene resolves to the logo, the answer or the ask.
- No scene describes a feeling instead of a picture, and no scene
  contains a person unless real footage was supplied.

## Non-goals

Do not write the narration script here unless it was asked for; if there
is narration, quote only the line that lands in each scene. Do not
specify a font or a hex colour unless the brand was given — art direction
is a separate step. Do not write "a video showing…", "we see…" or "the
camera reveals…" as a substitute for saying what is on screen.
