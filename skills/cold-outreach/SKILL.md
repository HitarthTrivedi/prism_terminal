## The job

The reader owes you nothing, is on a phone, and decides in the subject
line and the first sentence. Everything below follows from those two
facts. The goal of a first email is a reply, not a sale.

## Rules

1. **Under 120 words.** Count them. A long first email is deleted
   unread, and length reads as "this was sent to a thousand people".
2. **The first line is about them, and it is verifiable.** Something you
   actually found: a plant they opened, a tender they won, a product on
   their site, a standard they work to. Not "I hope this finds you well"
   and not "I came across your profile".
3. **No claim you cannot evidence.** No percentages, multiples or
   savings figures unless they came from the approved claims list or from
   the person's own material. An invented "we cut costs 30%" is the fastest
   way to lose the account and the sender's domain reputation.
4. **One ask, and make it small.** A fifteen-minute call, or a yes/no
   question. Never "let me know your thoughts on the attached deck".
5. **Say who you are in one clause, not a paragraph.** They will look you
   up if the first line earned it.
6. **Subject line: four to seven words, lower case, no punctuation
   tricks.** It says what the email is about. No "Quick question", no
   "Re:", no emoji, nothing that pretends to be a reply.
7. **Write as one person to one person.** "We are a leading provider" is
   a brochure. "I build conveyor structures in Ahmedabad" is a person.
8. **Only one placeholder: the name.** If a merge field cannot be filled
   from the row, the sentence is rewritten, not sent with a gap.
9. **Nothing attached on a first email.** Attachments to strangers go to
   spam and are not opened.

## Shape

```
SUBJECT: <4–7 words, lower case>
BODY:
<One line about them, specific and checkable.>
<One or two lines: what you do, and why that connects to the line above.>
<The ask, in one sentence.>
<Sign-off, name, company, one contact line.>
```

## Quality checklist

- The body is under 120 words.
- The first sentence names something specific to this recipient.
- Every number in the email came from the claims list or their own
  material. If there is no such source, there is no number.
- There is exactly one ask and it costs the reader under fifteen minutes.
- The subject is four to seven words and does not pretend to be a reply.
- No placeholder other than the recipient's name.
- No attachment, no link farm, at most one link.

## Non-goals

Do not write a follow-up sequence unless asked — this is the first email.
Do not describe your product's features; the reply is where that happens.
Do not use "I hope this email finds you well", "reaching out", "touch
base", "circle back", "synergy" or "game-changer".
